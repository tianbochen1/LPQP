# LPQP	
The power $p$ in an $L_p$-quantile score determines which features of serial dependence appear in its spectrum. At the median level, a latent-state construction links spectral visibility to a signed fractional moment of the emission distribution. We construct processes with flat score spectra at $p=1$ and $p=2$ but nonflat spectra at every interior power, and processes with any prescribed finite set of invisible powers in $[1,2]$. Under a bounded-support condition, cancellation powers are isolated unless all powers are invisible. We also characterize how independent symmetric replacement changes signal strength. A permutation scan combines finite-lag statistics across powers and controls the finite-sample level under exchangeability. Simulations show when scanning recovers dependence missed by the endpoints and when it loses power relative to a fixed choice. Applications to western Danish electricity prices and Bitcoin returns show how empirical comparisons across powers depend on preprocessing and calibration.
